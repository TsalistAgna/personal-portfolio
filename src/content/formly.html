Complex forms. Clearer flow.

A case-data entry concept that brings intake notes, conditional fields, and structured information into one focused workspace.

Project Overview

I designed Formly to explore a more manageable way to complete detailed case information: identity, family relationships, employment or school history, and contact details. The workspace combines a notes-to-form concept with structured manual entry, with conditional sections shaped by marital status and relevant relationship history.

This is a visual and interaction design concept. AI-assisted filling is a proposed interaction shown in the design; extraction, validation, and submission have not been implemented or tested.

Make a complex form easier to work through.

The design challenge

When every possible question competes for attention, a long form becomes difficult to scan. Some questions only become relevant after an earlier answer, while repeated relationship and employment records add another layer of complexity.

I framed the design around one question: how might we help users enter detailed case information while keeping relevant questions, progress, and next steps clear?

Interface Direction

I used sage-green surfaces to distinguish the workspace from white form cards. Grouped sections give the long form a hierarchy, while the sidebar separates workspace-level actions from the current data-entry task.

Search and field navigation offer alternatives to scrolling. Progress is framed around applicable fields, so completion relates to the current case. Consistent typography, controls, spacing, and corner treatments connect the different states.

Show the questions that matter next.

I explored single, divorced, and married variations rather than presenting every relationship question at once. The divorced variations account for different ways a marriage ended; married variations include previous marriages and a current spouse’s prior relationship history.

Conditional disclosure is intended to keep irrelevant information out of the immediate task while leaving room for complex cases. The design explores these variations without establishing a verified set of branching or validation rules.

Key Design Decisions

  • Grouped information: I organised related questions into recognisable sections so the structure of the case is visible within the form.
  • Conditional disclosure: Additional questions appear when earlier answers make them relevant, keeping the immediate task focused.
  • Applicable-field progress: Completion is framed around the fields relevant to the current case, rather than every possible question.
  • Notes-to-form assistance: I explored an AI-assisted starting point to reduce repeated manual entry. Extracted information would need verification in a working product.
  • Support for repeated records: Relationship and employment histories can extend beyond a single entry in the designed form.
  • Consistent interface foundations: Shared visual rules connect form states and screen sizes without requiring users to relearn the interface.

Mobile Exploration

The Figma design also includes a mobile exploration. It considers a smaller-screen presentation of the case-entry task; the complete desktop flow has not been implemented or validated on mobile.

Reflection & Next Steps

Formly is an exploration of how information structure and interface states can work together. My design decisions establish a direction for a complex form; they do not yet demonstrate improved completion, accuracy, or usability.

Before turning the concept into a working product, I would:

  • Test whether users understand the conditional questions.
  • Validate how progress updates when applicable fields change.
  • Evaluate navigation through long and repeated sections.
  • Design and test review, correction, and uncertainty handling for AI-filled information.
  • Check keyboard navigation, focus order, labels, and error messages.
  • Review responsive behaviour across the full form.
  • Define appropriate privacy and data-handling requirements for a real implementation.
